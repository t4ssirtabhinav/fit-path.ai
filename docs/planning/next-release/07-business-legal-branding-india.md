# Research: Company, Domain & Branding Registration — Legalities for a Newcomer (India)

Covers ticket **#13** — "Company, domain, branding registration — legalities for a newbie, avoiding legal/copyright trouble, initial/running costs, licensing."

Written for a first-time founder in India with no prior company-formation experience.

---

## 1. Choosing a company structure — the foundational decision
| Structure | What it is | Best fit for | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **One Person Company (OPC)** | A company with a single owner, but with limited liability (unlike a sole proprietorship) | A solo founder who wants legal separation between personal and business liability without bringing in co-founders yet | Simplest "real company" structure for exactly FitPath's likely current situation |
| **Limited Liability Partnership (LLP)** | Partnership structure with limited liability for partners | Two or more founders who want flexibility and lower compliance burden than a private limited company | Cannot easily raise equity funding — a real constraint if external investment is part of the plan |
| **Private Limited Company (Pvt Ltd)** | The standard startup structure — shares, a board, formal governance | Founders planning to raise external funding (VCs/angels generally require this structure) | Higher compliance burden (more filings, audits) but is the **prerequisite structure for most India startup funding programs** referenced in doc 08 of this folder |

**Recommendation**: if there's *any* realistic chance of seeking external funding (angel/VC, or applying to the accelerator/grant programs covered in doc 08), **register as a Private Limited Company from the start** — converting from OPC/LLP to Pvt Ltd later is possible but adds cost, delay, and friction at exactly the moment momentum matters most. If funding is firmly not the plan, OPC is the simpler and cheaper starting point.

## 2. The registration process (SPICe+)
India's company registration is consolidated through the **SPICe+ (Simplified Proforma for Incorporating a Company Electronically Plus)** form via the **Ministry of Corporate Affairs (MCA)** portal — a single integrated form covering name reservation, incorporation, PAN/TAN allotment, EPFO/ESIC registration, and a bank account in one filing. This consolidation is a genuine improvement over the older fragmented process and is the standard path recommended for new companies. Practical steps:
1. Obtain a **Digital Signature Certificate (DSC)** for proposed directors — required to file electronically.
2. **Reserve a company name** through the SPICe+ Part A name-check (search the MCA database first to avoid a rejected reservation — a common first-time pitfall).
3. File **SPICe+ Part B** with incorporation documents (MOA, AOA, registered office proof, identity/address proofs of directors).
4. Receive the **Certificate of Incorporation**, PAN, and TAN — typically issued together once approved.

## 3. Trademark registration — protecting "FitPath" as a brand
- Trademark registration in India is handled by the **Office of the Controller General of Patents, Designs and Trademarks**, via the IP India portal.
- **MSME/Udyam-registered startups get a meaningful fee concession**: the trademark application fee is **₹4,500 per class** for MSME/individual/startup applicants, versus **₹9,000 per class** for other companies — roughly half price. **Registering for Udyam/MSME status before filing the trademark is a concrete, low-effort cost-saving step** worth doing as a matter of sequence, not an afterthought.
- **Search before you file.** Use the IP India public search tools to confirm "FitPath" (and close variants/the logo) isn't already registered or pending in the relevant class (likely Class 9 for software/apps, possibly Class 41/44 for fitness/health services) — filing a name that's already taken wastes the fee and the months-long processing time.
- Trademark processing realistically takes **many months to over a year** in India — start this process **early and in parallel** with other formation steps, not after the product is already public-facing, to minimize the window where the brand is exposed but unprotected.

## 4. Domain registration and avoiding copyright/branding trouble
- **Domain registration** itself is straightforward and cheap (any standard registrar) — the actual risk is **not checking for conflicts before committing to a brand identity**:
  - Search trademark databases (as above) *and* do a basic web/app-store search for existing "FitPath"-named products before finalizing the name, logo, and domain together as one identity package — a name clash discovered post-launch is far more expensive to fix than one caught in week one.
  - **Don't use stock imagery, fonts, icons, or any third-party content without verifying license terms** — many "free" asset sources restrict commercial use, and fitness apps are visually asset-heavy (icons, illustrations, exercise media — which directly intersects with the licensing question raised in doc 05 of this folder regarding exercise-content datasets).
- **Register domain + trademark + company name as one coordinated identity** rather than three separate decisions made at different times — this is the single most common sequencing mistake first-time founders make (ending up with a company name, a domain, and a trademark that don't quite match).

## 5. Costs — realistic first-year planning figures
| Item | Approximate cost | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Company incorporation (Pvt Ltd, via SPICe+, with professional/CA assistance) | A few thousand to ~₹15,000-25,000 in professional fees, plus government filing fees (which scale with authorized capital) | DIY filing is possible but risky for a first-timer — a CA/company-secretary's fee is usually worth it to avoid costly filing mistakes |
| Digital Signature Certificate (DSC) | A few hundred to ~₹2,000 per director | One-time per director |
| Trademark filing (MSME/startup rate) | **₹4,500 per class** | Register Udyam/MSME status first to access this rate |
| Domain registration | A few hundred to ~₹1,000/year | Routine, recurring |
| Annual compliance (Pvt Ltd: ROC filings, audits, etc.) | Meaningfully higher than OPC/LLP — budget for an accountant/CA on retainer | This **recurring** cost is the real long-term consideration in the structure choice from §1, not just the one-time incorporation cost |

## 6. Practical recommendations, in sequence
1. **Register Udyam/MSME status first** — it's free, fast, and immediately unlocks the discounted trademark fee plus other startup-scheme eligibility (ties directly into doc 08's funding research).
2. **Run the name/trademark/domain conflict search before finalizing any branding** — cheapest possible point to catch a problem.
3. **Engage a CA or company-secretary for the actual incorporation filing** — the cost is modest relative to the risk of filing errors that cause delays or require costly corrections.
4. **File the trademark application early and in parallel** with incorporation — its multi-month timeline means starting late directly extends the brand's exposure window.
5. **Treat asset licensing as a checklist item for every visual/content asset** that goes into the product — logos, icons, exercise media, stock photos — before they ship, not after a complaint arrives.
